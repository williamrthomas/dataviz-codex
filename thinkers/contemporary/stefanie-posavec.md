# Stefanie Posavec (~1981--present)
> "Making visualization by hand creates a different relationship between maker, data, and viewer than screen-based tools allow."

## Overview

Stefanie Posavec is the co-creator of *Dear Data*, a pioneer of handcrafted, non-digital data visualization, and a practitioner who explores the aesthetics of data as art. Originally from the US and based in the UK, Posavec has been active from the 2000s to the present, producing work that treats text, language, and personal experience as data to be visualized through physical, hand-drawn media.

Posavec's work represents a distinctive position in the field: she insists that the medium of visualization matters, and that hand-drawing carries connotations of care, labor, and individuality that digital tools suppress. Her practice connects data visualization to the broader traditions of craft, drawing, and artist's books.

## Key Contributions

- Co-created the **Dear Data** project (with [Giorgia Lupi](./giorgia-lupi.md)), a year-long postcard data correspondence now in the permanent collection of the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA)
- Pioneered **handcrafted, non-digital data visualization** as a distinct practice and aesthetic
- Explored the visualization of **text and language as data**, treating literary works as datasets
- Demonstrated that the **medium of visualization** (hand-drawn vs. digital) shapes meaning and reception
- Co-authored *Observe, Collect, Draw!*, making personal data collection and hand-drawn visualization accessible to a broad audience

## Major Works

- *Dear Data* (with [Giorgia Lupi](./giorgia-lupi.md), 2016) -- 52 weeks of hand-drawn data postcards exchanged across the Atlantic
- *Observe, Collect, Draw!* (with [Lupi](./giorgia-lupi.md), 2018) -- a personal data journal and activity book
- Literary and text-based data visualizations, including visualizations of Jack Kerouac's *On the Road* and other literary works
- Commissioned data art installations and exhibitions

## Core Philosophy

Posavec believes that making visualization by hand -- drawing it, feeling it -- creates a different relationship between maker, data, and viewer than screen-based tools allow. The medium of hand-drawing carries connotations of care, labor, and individuality that digital tools suppress.

This is not a Luddite rejection of technology but a considered argument about how materiality shapes meaning. When a viewer encounters a hand-drawn visualization, they understand intuitively that a human being spent time and attention on every mark -- that the visualization is an artifact of care. This changes the viewer's relationship to the data: it becomes more intimate, more personal, more connected to human experience.

Posavec's practice of treating text and language as data extends the boundaries of what can be visualized. Her literary data visualizations reveal patterns in writing style, sentence structure, and word choice that readers experience unconsciously but rarely see made visible.

## Connections

- Close collaborator with [Giorgia Lupi](./giorgia-lupi.md) on *Dear Data* and *Observe, Collect, Draw!*; together they are the most visible practitioners of the [data humanism school](../../schools/data-humanism.md)
- Her work connects to the [artistic/expressive school](../../schools/artistic-expressive.md) of visualization
- The handcraft emphasis connects her practice to [W.E.B. Du Bois's](../historical/web-du-bois.md) hand-drawn data visualizations, which also carried meaning through their materiality and labor
- Represents a counter-position to the digital-first approach of [Mike Bostock](./mike-bostock.md) and the D3/web visualization tradition
- Her literary data visualizations share [Jacques Bertin's](../historical/jacques-bertin.md) interest in visual encoding but apply it to unconventional data domains
- The *Dear Data* project's emphasis on personal narrative connects to the [narrative storytelling school](../../schools/narrative-storytelling.md)

## Influence

The *Dear Data* project (52 weeks of postcards exchanged across the Atlantic, each one a hand-drawn visualization of personal data) became a celebrated example of artistic data visualization and is now in the permanent collection of the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA). Its acquisition validated hand-drawn data visualization as a serious artistic and intellectual practice.

The project influenced the maker/craft dimension of the data art movement, inspiring countless practitioners to pick up pens and paper and visualize their own personal data. It demonstrated that data visualization does not require code, software, or technical expertise -- that the most powerful data stories can be told with the simplest tools.

Posavec's literary data visualizations opened a new domain for visualization practice, demonstrating that text and language are rich data sources that reward visual exploration. Her work has been exhibited in galleries and museums internationally, establishing data visualization as a recognized medium within contemporary art.

*Observe, Collect, Draw!* extended the *Dear Data* ethos into an interactive format, inviting readers to become data collectors and visualization makers themselves -- democratizing the practice of personal data visualization.

## Key Quotes

- "Making visualization by hand creates a different relationship between maker, data, and viewer than screen-based tools allow."
- "The medium carries meaning. A hand-drawn line says something different from a computer-generated one."
- "Data visualization does not require code. It requires attention."
