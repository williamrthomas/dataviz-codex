# Stephen Few (active 2000s--present)
> "Visualization is about communication, and effective communication is measurable."

## Overview

Stephen Few is a leading practitioner voice for perceptual and analytical rigor in business dashboards and reports. As the founder of Perceptual Edge consultancy and author of the definitive books on dashboard design, Few has established the standard reference framework for business intelligence and dashboard practitioners. His work is characterized by a strongly scientific position: visualization is about communication, and the effectiveness of that communication can -- and should -- be measured.

Few's influence is concentrated in the enterprise and business intelligence space, where his books *Show Me the Numbers*, *Information Dashboard Design*, and *Now You See It* have become canonical texts. His 7-criteria "Data Visualization Effectiveness Profile" provides a systematic evaluation rubric that moves visualization quality assessment from subjective opinion to structured analysis. Few is a key figure in the [scientific-perceptual school](../../schools/scientific-perceptual.md), carrying forward the research-grounded tradition of [William Cleveland](../modern/william-cleveland.md) into the world of business practice.

## Core Philosophy

Few takes a strongly scientific position: visualization is about communication, and effective communication is measurable. This means that visualization design choices should be guided by evidence about how human visual perception works -- not by aesthetic fashion, creative impulse, or decorative tradition. Every element in a visualization should earn its place by contributing to the reader's understanding.

He rejects [chartjunk](../../principles/chartjunk.md), decorative elements, and aesthetic experimentation that sacrifices clarity. In this, he is a direct intellectual heir of [Edward Tufte](../modern/edward-tufte.md)'s emphasis on [data-ink ratio](../../principles/data-ink-ratio.md) and the elimination of non-data elements. But where Tufte operates primarily as a critic and theorist, Few translates these principles into concrete, actionable guidelines for business practitioners designing dashboards and reports.

His 7-criteria Data Visualization Effectiveness Profile provides a systematic evaluation rubric:
1. **Informative** -- does it communicate meaningful information?
2. **Usefulness** -- is the information relevant to the audience's needs?
3. **Perceptibility** -- can the information be perceived accurately and efficiently?
4. **Truthfulness** -- does it represent the data honestly?
5. **Intuitiveness** -- can it be understood without extensive instruction?
6. **Aesthetics** -- is it visually appealing without sacrificing function?
7. **Engagement** -- does it draw and hold the viewer's attention?

This framework operationalizes the principles of the [scientific-perceptual school](../../schools/scientific-perceptual.md), making them applicable to everyday design decisions in enterprise contexts.

## Influence

Few's books are the standard reference for business intelligence and dashboard practitioners. In an industry often driven by vendor marketing and feature checklists, Few provided an evidence-based framework for evaluating visualization quality that has shaped how thousands of organizations design their dashboards and reports.

His Data Visualization Effectiveness Profile has been widely adopted as an evaluation tool in corporate settings, university courses, and design reviews. By providing a structured rubric rather than subjective guidelines, Few made it possible for non-designers to assess and improve their visualization work systematically.

He is a key figure in the [scientific-perceptual school](../../schools/scientific-perceptual.md), alongside Cleveland, carrying the tradition of evidence-based visualization design from academic research into the world of business practice. His influence is particularly strong in enterprise software, healthcare analytics, financial reporting, and other domains where clarity and accuracy are paramount.

Few's consistent, decades-long argument against [chartjunk](../../principles/chartjunk.md) and decorative visualization has helped maintain a countervailing voice in a field that sometimes prizes visual novelty over communicative effectiveness.
