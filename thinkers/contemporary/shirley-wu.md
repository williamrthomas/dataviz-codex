# Shirley Wu (1988--present)
> "I start with curiosity rather than data -- I ask what I want to understand about the world, then find the data, then find the form."

## Overview

Shirley Wu is a creative data visualization practitioner based in the United States, known for producing some of the most inventive and emotionally resonant visualizations in the contemporary field. Her work ranges from the celebrated "An Interactive Visualization of Every Line in Hamilton" to "Film Flowers," which renders movie genres as botanical forms. She co-authored *Data Sketches* with [Nadieh Bremer](./nadieh-bremer.md), and has increasingly moved into physical and tactile data art -- installations and sculptures that expand the field's concept of what data visualization can be.

Wu's approach inverts the typical data visualization workflow. Where most practitioners begin with a dataset and ask how to visualize it, Wu begins with curiosity -- a question about the world -- and then seeks the data and the form that will answer it. This curiosity-first methodology produces work that is distinctive in its emotional and experiential richness.

## Key Contributions

- **Pioneered curiosity-driven visualization practice** -- starting with questions rather than datasets, producing work with unusual emotional depth
- **Co-created the Data Sketches project** -- a year-long collaborative creative exercise with Nadieh Bremer that became a model for the field
- **Expanded visualization into physical and multi-sensory media** -- pushing the field beyond screens into installations, sculptures, and tactile experiences
- **Demonstrated D3.js as an expressive medium** -- using JavaScript and D3 as creative tools rather than just chart-generation utilities
- **Created iconic individual visualizations** -- "Hamilton" and "Film Flowers" are among the most recognized creative visualizations of the 2010s

## Major Works

- *Data Sketches* (with Nadieh Bremer, 2021)
- "Film Flowers" -- visualization of movie genres as botanical forms
- "An Interactive Visualization of Every Line in Hamilton" -- every line in the musical, visualized by character and sentiment
- Physical data art installations and sculptures (ongoing)

## Core Philosophy

Wu starts with curiosity rather than data. She asks what she wants to understand about the world, then finds the data, then finds the form. This approach produces visualizations that feel personal and emotionally grounded in a way that data-first workflows rarely achieve.

She is deeply interested in the emotional and experiential dimensions of visualization -- not just what information a visualization conveys, but how it *feels* to encounter it. This interest has driven her push into physical, multi-sensory data art: installations and sculptures that engage the body as well as the eye. Her work suggests that data visualization's future may extend well beyond the screen.

Wu's philosophy aligns with the [data humanism](../../schools/data-humanism.md) movement championed by [Giorgia Lupi](./giorgia-lupi.md), emphasizing the human, emotional, and experiential dimensions of data. But Wu's distinctive contribution is the expansion into physical media -- asserting that data can be touched, inhabited, and experienced spatially.

## Influence

Wu's work exemplifies the creative coding approach -- using D3 and JavaScript as expressive media rather than just chart-generation tools. Her shift into physical data art (installations, sculptures) is expanding the field's concept of what "data visualization" can be, suggesting that the medium's future extends beyond screens and browsers into the physical world.

Her "Hamilton" and "Film Flowers" visualizations demonstrated that data visualization can engage popular culture audiences who might never seek out a statistical chart. The *Data Sketches* collaboration with Bremer established a model of deliberate creative practice that continues to inspire practitioners worldwide.
