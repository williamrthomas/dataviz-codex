# Stephanie Evergreen (active 2000s--present)
> "Researchers and evaluators who communicate data clearly are doing ethical work -- they respect their audiences' time and intelligence, and they enable better decisions."

## Overview

Stephanie Evergreen is a pioneering voice for effective visualization in nonprofit, evaluation, and organizational research contexts. Active from the 2000s to the present, she has developed practical tools and guides that bridge research evidence with day-to-day visualization practice, particularly for audiences who have high data literacy but historically poor graphical literacy.

Evergreen's work targets a critical gap in the visualization field: the vast community of researchers, evaluators, and organizational professionals who work with data daily but have had little formal training in visual communication. Her Data Visualization Checklist and practical guides have become standard resources in these communities.

## Key Contributions

- Developed the **Data Visualization Checklist**, a widely used online evaluation tool for assessing visualization quality
- Authored practical guides that bridge research evidence with day-to-day visualization practice
- Pioneered **audience-first** visualization methodology for nonprofit and evaluation contexts
- Brought evidence-based visualization design to the **evaluation, public health, and social science** communities
- Created training programs and workshops that have reached thousands of organizational professionals
- Advocated for the ethical dimension of clear data communication

## Major Works

- *Effective Data Visualization: The Right Chart for the Right Data* (2017, 2nd edition 2019)
- *Presenting Data Effectively* (2014, 2nd edition 2018)
- The Data Visualization Checklist (online tool)
- Evergreen Data blog and training programs

## Core Philosophy

Evergreen's central argument is that researchers and evaluators who communicate data clearly are doing ethical work -- they respect their audiences' time and intelligence, and they enable better decisions. Poor visualization is not merely an aesthetic failure; it is a failure of responsibility to one's audience and one's data.

Her approach is **audience-first**: start with what people need to know, then choose the form that communicates it most clearly. This inverts the common workflow of starting with data and tools, placing the communication goal at the center of every design decision.

Evergreen's work is deeply practical -- she focuses on the tools people actually use (Excel, PowerPoint, Google Slides) and shows how to apply evidence-based design principles within those constraints. This pragmatism has been essential to reaching communities that do not have access to specialized visualization software.

## Connections

- Shares [Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic's](./cole-nussbaumer-knaflic.md) emphasis on audience-first design and practical accessibility, though Evergreen focuses more on research and evaluation contexts while Knaflic targets business audiences
- Draws on [Edward Tufte's](../modern/edward-tufte.md) principles of data-ink ratio and chart simplification, translated for organizational settings
- Applies [William Cleveland's](../modern/william-cleveland.md) perceptual science findings to practical chart-choice guidance
- Aligned with the [narrative storytelling school](../../schools/narrative-storytelling.md) in her emphasis on clear communication
- Complements [Alberto Cairo's](./alberto-cairo.md) ethical framework with a practitioner-oriented approach to the same values
- Part of a broader movement (with Knaflic, Cairo, and others) to democratize evidence-based visualization design

## Influence

Evergreen's work has had particular impact in the evaluation, public health, and social science communities -- audiences who have high data literacy but historically poor graphical literacy. These communities produce enormous volumes of reports, presentations, and data summaries, and Evergreen has been instrumental in raising the visual quality of this output.

Her Data Visualization Checklist is widely used in organizational settings as a practical quality-assurance tool, providing a concrete and actionable set of criteria that anyone can apply to their visualizations. It has been adopted by government agencies, nonprofits, foundations, and research organizations.

Evergreen represents a crucial bridge between the academic visualization literature and the millions of practitioners who create charts in spreadsheets and slide decks every day. Her contribution is not new theory but effective translation -- making existing knowledge accessible and actionable for the communities that need it most.
