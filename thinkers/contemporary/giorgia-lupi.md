# Giorgia Lupi (~1981--present)
> "Data is never cold or objective. It is always the result of human decisions about what to count, how to count it, and what to leave out."

## Overview

Giorgia Lupi is the pioneer of "data humanism," co-creator of the celebrated *Dear Data* project, design partner at Pentagram, and one of the most influential advocates for visualization that captures the personal, emotional, and imprecise dimensions of human experience. Originally from Italy and now based in the US, Lupi has been active from the 2010s to the present.

Lupi represents a deliberate counter-position to the dominant tradition of precision-oriented, objectivity-focused data visualization. Her work argues that data is always partial, always made by humans, always carrying context and imprecision -- and that visualization should embrace, rather than hide, these qualities. She seeks visualizations that feel *human*: warm, personal, uncertain, and emotional.

## Key Contributions

- Authored the **"Data Humanism" manifesto** (2014), establishing a recognized school of thought in visualization
- Co-created the **Dear Data** project (with [Stefanie Posavec](./stefanie-posavec.md)), a year-long postcard data correspondence that is now in the permanent collection of the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA)
- Established data humanism as a recognized school of thought and practice
- Advocated for visualization that embraces **personal data, uncertainty, and emotion**
- Design partner at **Pentagram**, bringing data visualization into one of the world's most prestigious design firms
- Challenged the assumption that data visualization must be cold, precise, and objective

## Major Works

- *Observe, Collect, Draw!* (with [Posavec](./stefanie-posavec.md), 2018) -- a personal data journal and activity book

## Core Philosophy

Lupi argues that the dominant tradition of data visualization has over-indexed on precision and objectivity, treating data as cold and complete. **Data humanism** insists that data is always partial, always made by humans, always carrying context and imprecision -- and that visualization should embrace, rather than hide, these qualities.

She seeks visualizations that feel *human*: warm, personal, uncertain, and emotional. This is not a rejection of rigor but an expansion of what visualization can express. Lupi's work suggests that the most meaningful insights often come from data that is messy, personal, and subjective -- the kind of data that conventional visualization practice tends to clean away or ignore.

Her approach challenges the separation between the maker and the data: in *Dear Data*, the act of collecting data about one's own life (how many times you smiled, what made you angry, how you spent your time) becomes an act of self-reflection, and the hand-drawn visualization becomes an intimate communication between two people.

## Connections

- Represents a deliberate counter-position to [Edward Tufte's](../modern/edward-tufte.md) [minimalist](../../schools/minimalist-tuftean.md) orthodoxy and the scientific visualization tradition
- Her intellectual lineage traces through [W.E.B. Du Bois](../historical/web-du-bois.md) (data as political and personal) and Isotype/Neurath (data as democratic communication)
- Close collaborator with [Stefanie Posavec](./stefanie-posavec.md) on *Dear Data* and *Observe, Collect, Draw!*
- Founder and primary theorist of the [data humanism school](../../schools/data-humanism.md)
- Her work connects to the [artistic/expressive school](../../schools/artistic-expressive.md) of visualization
- Shares [Alberto Cairo's](./alberto-cairo.md) conviction that visualization is never neutral, though her response is to embrace subjectivity rather than pursue objectivity more rigorously
- Her personal data approach influenced how practitioners think about qualitative data visualization and the relationship between data and empathy

## Influence

Data humanism has become a recognized school of thought in the visualization field, and Lupi is its primary theorist and most visible practitioner. Her influence extends in several directions:

The *Dear Data* project (52 weeks of postcards exchanged across the Atlantic, each one a hand-drawn visualization of personal data) became one of the most celebrated examples of artistic data visualization. Its acquisition by MoMA validated data visualization as a form of art and personal expression.

Lupi's approach has influenced how practitioners think about personal data, qualitative data visualization, and the relationship between data and empathy. In corporate, nonprofit, and journalism settings, her ideas have encouraged designers to consider the human stories behind datasets and to find visual forms that honor complexity and ambiguity.

Her work at Pentagram has brought data visualization practice into the mainstream of graphic design, demonstrating that data-driven work belongs alongside branding, typography, and editorial design at the highest levels of the profession.

## Key Quotes
