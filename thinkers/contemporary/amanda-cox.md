# Amanda Cox (active 2000s--present)
> "The goal is to get something in front of readers that they can understand immediately, that respects their intelligence, and that is honest about what the data shows."

## Overview

Amanda Cox is one of the originators of modern data journalism, described as "the Michael Phelps of infographics." She served as editor of The Upshot at the *New York Times* and is currently executive editor of data journalism at Bloomberg News. Active from the 2000s to the present, Cox has defined what data journalism can be in the digital age.

Under Cox's influence, the NYT's graphics department became the gold standard for data journalism worldwide -- a template that virtually every serious news organization now follows. Her work has demonstrated that data visualization in journalism can be simultaneously sophisticated, accessible, and honest, reaching millions of readers while maintaining rigorous standards of accuracy and clarity.

## Key Contributions

- Pioneered the **modern data journalism** approach at the *New York Times*, defining the genre for an entire industry
- Led the creation of landmark interactive and statistical graphics, including **probabilistic election graphics** (the "election needle") and interactive explainers
- Served as editor of **The Upshot**, the NYT's data journalism vertical
- Pioneered the NYT's approach to **economic data storytelling**, making complex statistical concepts accessible to general audiences
- Currently serving as executive editor of data journalism at **Bloomberg News**
- Demonstrated that data journalism visualizations can be immediately comprehensible, contextually grounded, and honest about uncertainty

## Major Works

- "512 Paths to the White House" (2012) -- watershed interactive election graphic
- The NYT election needle -- pioneering probabilistic visualization for live election coverage
- Numerous *New York Times* interactive data journalism pieces
- Leadership of The Upshot data journalism team
- Data journalism leadership at Bloomberg News (current)

## Core Philosophy

Cox believes that data journalism visualizations must serve readers who are not specialists -- graphics must be **immediately comprehensible**, **contextually grounded**, and **honest about uncertainty**. This triad of values -- accessibility, context, and honesty -- defines her approach to data communication.

Her pioneering work on probabilistic graphics, particularly the election needle, represents a willingness to show uncertainty to general audiences rather than presenting false certainty. This was controversial (the needle provoked anxiety in viewers during election coverage) but intellectually honest, and it advanced the public conversation about how to communicate statistical uncertainty.

Cox's approach emphasizes that data journalism is journalism first: the goal is not to showcase technical virtuosity but to help readers understand the world. Every design decision is made in service of the reader's comprehension, not the designer's skill.

## Connections

- Worked alongside [Mike Bostock](./mike-bostock.md) at the *New York Times*, forming one of the most influential data journalism teams in the world. Cox provided the journalistic vision; Bostock provided the technical infrastructure
- Her journalistic ethic complements Bostock's technical ambition
- Central figure in the [journalistic school](../../schools/journalistic.md) of data visualization
- Shares [Alberto Cairo's](./alberto-cairo.md) commitment to truthful, ethical visualization, applied in the context of daily deadline journalism
- Her emphasis on accessibility and audience connects to [Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic's](./cole-nussbaumer-knaflic.md) audience-first methodology, though Cox operates in a journalistic rather than business context
- Draws on [Edward Tufte's](../modern/edward-tufte.md) principles of clarity and data density, adapted for the constraints and opportunities of web-based journalism
- Her work on uncertainty visualization connects to [William Cleveland's](../modern/william-cleveland.md) statistical graphics tradition
- The NYT graphics team she helped build used [D3.js](./mike-bostock.md) as its primary visualization technology

## Influence

The NYT's graphics department under Cox's influence defined what data journalism could be -- a template that The Guardian, FiveThirtyEight, The Economist, the Washington Post, and virtually every serious news organization now follows. Before Cox and her colleagues, data journalism was a niche practice; after them, it became a core competency that every major newsroom now invests in.

Specific graphics produced under her leadership became watershed moments in interactive journalism. "512 Paths to the White House" demonstrated how interactive visualization could make complex conditional probability accessible to a mass audience. The election needle showed that it was possible -- and arguably necessary -- to communicate uncertainty to the public rather than presenting false precision.

Cox's move from the NYT to Bloomberg News signals the continued expansion of data journalism beyond traditional news organizations, as financial media invests in sophisticated data communication for its audiences.

Her influence extends beyond specific graphics to the institutional model she helped create: the integrated data journalism team that includes reporters, designers, and developers working together to produce work that is simultaneously journalistic, visual, and technically sophisticated.

## Key Quotes

- "The purpose of a graphic is not to be pretty. It is to help people understand something."
- "The best graphics are the ones where readers learn something they did not expect."
- "Uncertainty is not a failure of data. It is a feature of reality, and we should show it."
