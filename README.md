# The DataViz Codex

> "Above all else show the data."
> — Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* (1983)

A living codex of data visualization: principles, pioneers, techniques, and tools — from Tufte to the frontier.

This repository is a structured knowledge base for anyone who works with data and wants to understand the intellectual tradition behind how we visualize it. It is opinionated but inclusive, practical but grounded in theory, and designed to be browsed, studied, and contributed to.

## Contents

### [Thinkers](thinkers/)
The pantheon of data visualization — 37 thinkers across three eras, each with a standalone profile covering their contributions, philosophy, major works, and connections to the broader field.

- [Historical Pioneers (pre-1950)](thinkers/historical/) — Graunt, Playfair, Nightingale, Minard, Snow, Du Bois, Neurath, Brinton, Bertin
- [Modern Founders (1950-2000)](thinkers/modern/) — Tufte, Tukey, Wainer, Cleveland, Mackinlay, Shneiderman, Card, Wilkinson, Friendly
- [Contemporary Leaders (2000-present)](thinkers/contemporary/) — Wickham, Bostock, Cairo, Munzner, Evergreen, Knaflic, Lupi, Posavec, Cox, Bremer, Wu, Viegas & Wattenberg, Yau, Kirk, Stefaner, Meeks, Rosling, Few, Heer

### [Principles](principles/)
Sixteen core concepts and techniques — from data-ink ratio to accessibility — each with definition, origin, examples, and practical guidance.

### [Schools of Thought](schools/)
Seven schools of visualization philosophy — Minimalist, Scientific/Perceptual, Grammar of Graphics, Narrative, Data Humanism, Artistic, and Journalistic — with their key figures, tenets, canonical texts, and productive tensions.

### [Library](library/)
The essential reading list, organized by level: beginner, intermediate, advanced, and reference. Every book includes context on why it matters and what to read first.

### [Case Studies](case-studies/)
Seven famous visualizations analyzed in depth — from Minard's Napoleon march to Tufte's Challenger critique — each as a self-contained analytical narrative.

### [Curriculum](curriculum/)
Four learning paths tailored to different backgrounds: Foundations (start here), Practitioner (working analysts), Designer (visual designers entering data viz), and Advanced (theory, research, frontier).

A focused study, [Digital Analytical Instruments](curriculum/digital-practice.md), connects this foundation to cartographic design, Rams and Ive, multidimensional exploration, and motion. It distinguishes documented ideas from proposed practice and includes three experiments for evaluating the extensions.

The [Visual Practice Workshop](workshop/README.md) includes a browsable reference atlas and an above-fold artifact contract for an Astro devshare. Hans Rosling and the Gapminder team are included as precedents for guided explanation and distributions behind averages.

## Philosophy

This codex is built on several convictions:

**The field has a rich intellectual history.** Data visualization is not a recent invention of the tech industry. It has a 400-year lineage of brilliant thinkers, and understanding that lineage makes you a better practitioner.

**Theory and practice are inseparable.** The best visualization work is informed by perceptual science, design principles, and critical thinking — not just tool proficiency.

**No single school has all the answers.** Tufte's minimalism, Cleveland's empiricism, Lupi's humanism, and Knaflic's storytelling all illuminate different facets of the same problem. This codex presents them all, with their tensions intact.

**Cross-referencing is the point.** Every thinker connects to principles, schools, and case studies. Every principle connects back to its originators. The knowledge graph is the product.
