# Reference Reading List

History, criticism, and special topics. These books are not meant to be read in sequence but consulted as needed. They round out a deep understanding of the field by providing historical context, alternative perspectives, and specialized knowledge.

**Prerequisites:** Varies by book. Some assume significant prior reading; others are accessible to anyone with curiosity about the field's history and culture.

---

## 1. A History of Data Visualization and Graphic Communication

| | |
|---|---|
| **Authors** | [Michael Friendly](../thinkers/modern/michael-friendly.md) & [Howard Wainer](../thinkers/modern/howard-wainer.md) |
| **Year** | 2021 |
| **School** | Historical (cross-cutting) |
| **Prerequisites** | Intermediate reading list recommended |

### Why This Book Is Essential

The authoritative scholarly history of data visualization, from [William Playfair's](../thinkers/historical/william-playfair.md) first line charts in the 1780s through [Florence Nightingale's](../thinkers/historical/florence-nightingale.md) polar area diagrams, [Minard's](../thinkers/historical/charles-joseph-minard.md) flow maps, and into the computational age. Friendly and Wainer bring a statistician's rigor to historical analysis, tracing how innovations in graphic representation co-evolved with innovations in data collection, statistics, and technology.

This book corrects many myths about visualization history and recovers forgotten pioneers. Essential for anyone who wants to understand where the field came from and how its current debates echo earlier ones.

### Key Topics Covered

- The golden age of statistical graphics (1850-1900)
- The modern dark age (1900-1950) when graphics fell out of academic favor
- The rise and fall and rise of visualization in statistics
- Biographical sketches of key historical figures
- How technological change shaped visualization possibilities

## 2. The Truthful Art

| | |
|---|---|
| **Author** | [Alberto Cairo](../thinkers/contemporary/alberto-cairo.md) |
| **Year** | 2016 |
| **School** | [Narrative / Storytelling](../schools/narrative-storytelling.md) |
| **Prerequisites** | *The Functional Art* or equivalent; basic statistics |

### Why This Book Is Essential

Advanced Cairo. Where *The Functional Art* introduces visualization thinking and *How Charts Lie* teaches critical reading, *The Truthful Art* digs into the statistical and ethical foundations of honest visualization. The book covers uncertainty, distributions, hypothesis testing, maps, and infographics with more depth and rigor than Cairo's other works.

This is the book for practitioners who have moved past the basics and want to ensure their visualizations are not just clear but truthful. Cairo's emphasis on statistical literacy as a prerequisite for good visualization is a corrective to the "make it pretty" school of thought.

### Key Topics Covered

- Statistical foundations for visualization (distributions, correlation, uncertainty)
- Mapping and geographic visualization
- Ethical responsibilities of visualization designers
- The relationship between data journalism and statistical literacy
- Uncertainty visualization techniques

## 3. Dear Data

| | |
|---|---|
| **Authors** | [Giorgia Lupi](../thinkers/contemporary/giorgia-lupi.md) & [Stefanie Posavec](../thinkers/contemporary/stefanie-posavec.md) |
| **Year** | 2016 |
| **School** | [Data Humanism](../schools/data-humanism.md) / [Artistic / Expressive](../schools/artistic-expressive.md) |
| **Prerequisites** | None (but richer after reading other schools first) |

### Why This Book Is Essential

The definitive artifact of the [data humanism](../schools/data-humanism.md) movement. For a year, Lupi (in New York) and Posavec (in London) tracked personal data weekly — doors they opened, times they felt envious, sounds they heard — and mailed each other hand-drawn data visualizations on postcards. The project is intimate, analog, and profoundly anti-algorithmic.

*Dear Data* matters because it demonstrates that data visualization does not have to be about dashboards, business metrics, or perceptual optimization. It can be personal, artistic, imperfect, and deeply human. The book is a necessary counterweight to the field's dominant techno-rationalist assumptions and a gateway into the [Data Humanism](../schools/data-humanism.md) school of thought.

### Key Contributions

- Hand-drawn data visualization as legitimate practice
- Personal data as subject matter
- The correspondence format as visualization framework
- A physical, analog alternative to digital-first design
- Foundation text for the Data Humanism manifesto

## 4. W.E.B. Du Bois's Data Portraits: Visualizing Black America

| | |
|---|---|
| **Editors** | Whitney Battle-Baptiste & Britt Rusert |
| **Year** | 2018 |
| **School** | [Data Humanism](../schools/data-humanism.md) / Historical |
| **Prerequisites** | None |

### Why This Book Is Essential

A historical recovery of the extraordinary data visualizations that [W.E.B. Du Bois](../thinkers/historical/web-du-bois.md) and his students created for the 1900 Paris Exposition. These hand-drawn charts — depicting the economic, social, and educational progress of Black Americans in the decades after emancipation — are striking in their visual sophistication, their political purpose, and their beauty. They predate Tufte by eighty years and anticipate many modern principles.

This book is essential for understanding visualization's social justice tradition. Du Bois used data visualization not as a neutral analytical tool but as an argument for human dignity. His work demonstrates that visualization has always been political, and that the best visualization work is motivated by a purpose beyond mere information display.

### Key Topics Covered

- The 1900 Paris Exposition visualizations in full color reproduction
- Du Bois's methodology and data sources
- The social and political context of the work
- Du Bois's influence on modern data visualization and design
- The intersection of data visualization, race, and social justice

## 5. D3.js in Action

| | |
|---|---|
| **Author** | [Elijah Meeks](../thinkers/contemporary/elijah-meeks.md) |
| **Year** | 2015 (1st ed.) / 2017 (2nd ed.) |
| **School** | Technical reference (supports all schools) |
| **Prerequisites** | JavaScript proficiency; HTML/CSS/SVG familiarity |

### Why This Book Is Essential

The most comprehensive guide to D3.js, the most powerful and flexible web visualization library. [Mike Bostock's](../thinkers/contemporary/mike-bostock.md) D3 is the foundation of most custom web-based data visualization, and Meeks's book is the definitive guide to using it effectively. The book covers the full D3 API — selections, scales, axes, transitions, layouts, geographic projections, and force simulations — with practical examples and clear explanations.

D3 is not the easiest tool to learn, but it is the most capable. If you need to build custom interactive visualizations for the web — beyond what Tableau, ggplot2, or chart libraries can offer — D3 is the standard, and this book is the best way in.

### Key Topics Covered

- D3 selections and data binding
- Scales, axes, and coordinate systems
- Transitions and animation
- Geographic visualization (projections, topojson)
- Network and hierarchical visualization
- Interactive techniques (brushing, zooming, tooltips)
- Integration with modern frameworks

## Thematic Connections

The reference list connects to the rest of the codex in several ways:

### History and Origins
- Friendly & Wainer trace the history that the [Historical Thinkers](../thinkers/historical/) profiles cover in depth
- Du Bois's work connects to the [Data Humanism](../schools/data-humanism.md) school and its emphasis on data as partial and contextual

### Critical Perspectives
- Cairo's *The Truthful Art* deepens the statistical foundations introduced in the [Beginner](beginner.md) and [Intermediate](intermediate.md) lists
- *Dear Data* challenges the assumptions of the [Minimalist](../schools/minimalist-tuftean.md) and [Scientific](../schools/scientific-perceptual.md) schools

### Technical Depth
- Meeks's D3 guide is the technical complement to the theoretical frameworks in the [Advanced](advanced.md) list
- D3 implements many of the [Grammar of Graphics](../schools/grammar-of-graphics.md) principles in a web context
