# The DataViz Library

A curated reading list of essential texts in data visualization, organized by level. Each entry includes context on why the book matters, which school of thought it represents, and what to read first.

## Reading Paths

- [Beginner](beginner.md) — Start here if you are new to data visualization
- [Intermediate](intermediate.md) — For practitioners ready to deepen their understanding
- [Advanced](advanced.md) — Theory, research, and foundational texts
- [Reference](reference.md) — History, criticism, and special topics

## How to Use This Library

The books are organized into four progressive tiers. You do not need to read every book at one level before moving to the next, but the levels reflect increasing depth and assumed background knowledge.

**Beginner** books assume no prior knowledge of data visualization theory. They are practical, accessible, and focused on building good habits from the start. If you are a business analyst, journalist, or designer encountering data visualization for the first time, start here.

**Intermediate** books assume you have made charts, thought about why some work and others do not, and are ready for more rigorous frameworks. This is where you encounter the canonical texts that define the field's vocabulary.

**Advanced** books are theoretical, academic, and foundational. They are the primary sources that intermediate books draw upon. Researchers, graduate students, and serious practitioners will find these indispensable.

**Reference** books cover history, criticism, and special topics. They are not meant to be read cover-to-cover but consulted as needed. They round out a deep understanding of the field.

## Reading Order Recommendations

For a focused path through the most essential texts, see the [Curriculum](../curriculum/README.md), which provides structured learning paths for different backgrounds.

### The Essential Five

If you only read five books, make them these:

1. *Storytelling with Data* (Knaflic, 2015) — [Beginner](beginner.md)
2. *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* (Tufte, 1983) — [Intermediate](intermediate.md)
3. *How Charts Lie* (Cairo, 2019) — [Beginner](beginner.md)
4. *The Grammar of Graphics* (Wilkinson, 1999/2005) — [Advanced](advanced.md)
5. *Semiology of Graphics* (Bertin, 1967/1983) — [Advanced](advanced.md)
