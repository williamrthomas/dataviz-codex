# Curriculum

Four learning paths tailored to different backgrounds and goals. Each path provides a structured sequence of readings, principles to study, case studies to analyze, and skills to develop.

## Paths

- [Foundations](foundations.md) — Core theory. Start here regardless of background.
- [Practitioner](practitioner.md) — For working data analysts ready to level up their visualization skills.
- [Designer](designer.md) — For visual designers entering the data visualization field.
- [Advanced](advanced.md) — Deep theory, research papers, and frontier topics.

## How to Use These Paths

**Start with Foundations.** Regardless of your background, the Foundations path covers the essential books, principles, case studies, and thinkers that form the shared vocabulary of the field. It takes approximately 4-6 weeks of focused reading.

**Then choose your specialization.** The Practitioner, Designer, and Advanced paths are not sequential — they are parallel tracks for different goals:

- **Practitioner** is for working data analysts, data scientists, and business intelligence professionals who want to level up their visualization output. It emphasizes tools, workflow, and applied principles.
- **Designer** is for visual designers, UX designers, and art directors who are entering the data visualization field. It emphasizes typography, color, aesthetic schools, and design-first projects.
- **Advanced** is for researchers, graduate students, and senior practitioners who want deep theoretical grounding and exposure to the research frontier.

You can follow more than one path. Many practitioners benefit from both the Practitioner and Designer tracks.

## Philosophy

These curriculum paths are opinionated. They reflect several convictions:

**Theory and practice are inseparable.** Every path includes both reading and doing. The Foundations path prioritizes reading because the vocabulary must come first. The Practitioner and Designer paths shift toward projects because skills require practice. The Advanced path returns to deep reading because theory requires sustained engagement.

**No single school has all the answers.** The Foundations path introduces the major schools and their tensions. The specialized paths go deeper into specific traditions. By the end of the Advanced path, you should be able to draw from any school as the situation demands.

**Cross-referencing is the point.** Every curriculum path links to books, principles, schools, thinkers, and case studies. Following these links is not optional — the connections between ideas are as important as the ideas themselves.

**You learn by teaching.** The Advanced path encourages teaching the Foundations path to others. Explaining a principle to someone else is the deepest test of whether you truly understand it.
